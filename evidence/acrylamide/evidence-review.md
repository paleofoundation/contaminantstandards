# Acrylamide in Food and Selected Consumer Products: Evidence Review

Evidence review, version 0.1, 2026-11-02

Status: DRAFT working evidence file for the Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS). Not reviewed by the standards owner. Not for certification use. Every number below carries a source tag [S#] that maps to `sources.csv` in this folder. Values that could not be opened and confirmed in a source are recorded as "not found".

## 1. Scope

- Analyte: acrylamide (CAS 79-06-1) [S9]. Glycidamide is mentioned only as context: EFSA describes epoxidation of acrylamide to glycidamide as the route considered to underlie genotoxicity and carcinogenicity [S16]. No glycidamide limits or occurrence data are compiled here.
- Food matrices: french fries, potato crisps/chips, bread, breakfast cereals, biscuits and crackers, coffee and coffee substitutes, baby foods and processed cereal-based foods for infants and young children. These match the food categories in Article 1(2) of Regulation (EU) 2017/2158 [S1].
- Consumer products, included only where a sourced numeric value exists: drinking water (residual acrylamide monomer from polyacrylamide treatment chemicals) [S13] [S22]; cosmetic products (residual acrylamide in polyacrylamides) [S23].
- Exclusions: occupational exposure, tobacco smoke, food contact materials, and human biomonitoring.

## 2. Key regulatory distinction

The EU values for acrylamide in food are **benchmark levels**, not maximum levels. Recital 10 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2158 calls benchmark levels performance indicators for checking whether mitigation measures work [S1]. Article 1(1) requires food business operators to apply mitigation measures "in view of achieving levels of acrylamide as low as reasonably achievable below the benchmark levels" [S1]. Recital 15 says maximum levels for acrylamide in certain foods should be considered separately under Regulation (EEC) No 315/93 [S1].

Current status check: a full-text search of the consolidated Regulation (EU) 2023/915 (version 19.08.2026, amended up to M11, Regulation (EU) 2026/1825) found no entry for acrylamide [S3]. The EUR-Lex record for 2017/2158 lists no amending act, and its review deadline under Article 5 was 11/12/2020 [S1]. As of 2026-11-02 this review found no EU maximum level for acrylamide in food.

## 6. Gaps

1. No legally binding EU maximum level for acrylamide in food was found as of the 19.08.2026 consolidation of Regulation (EU) 2023/915 [S3]. EU values are benchmark levels only [S1].
2. The US FDA has no numeric limit or action level for acrylamide in food [S5]. No Codex numeric limit was found [S8], and no Health Canada numeric maximum level was found [S15].
3. Summary statistics for the FDA 2011 to 2015 survey (Abt et al. 2019) are behind a paywall and were not accessed [S7]. The FDA raw per-product tables were not aggregated because this review does not compute statistics.
4. No EFSA occurrence summary newer than the 2015 opinion was located or opened, and the EFSA data cover samples from 2010 onward [S16]. More recent EU-wide occurrence data are a gap.
5. EN 16618 and ISO 18862 figures come from catalogue scope records, not from the purchased standard texts [S19] [S20].
6. No AOAC Official Method for acrylamide was located.
7. Prop 65 settlement terms come from an Attorney General press release, not from the consent judgments [S12]. Other Prop 65 acrylamide settlements (for example cereals or cookies) were not reviewed.
8. The cosmetics entry was checked only in the original 2009 text [S23].
9. Public US laboratory price lists for acrylamide were not found.
10. Mesías et al. 2025 report the 2025 range as "<15 to 569 µg/kg" in the abstract and Section 3.1, but as "from <15 μg/kg to 337 μg/kg" in Section 3.3 [S17]. The discrepancy is unresolved.
