# Phthalates: Evidence Review

Evidence review, version 0.1, 2026-11-02

Status: DRAFT working evidence file for the Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS). Not reviewed by the standards owner. Not for certification use. Every number carries an [S#] tag that maps to `sources.csv` in this folder.

## 2. Scope

- Class: ortho-phthalates (slug `phthalates`).
- Analytes in scope: DEHP, DBP (also written DnBP), BBP (also written BBzP), DINP, DIDP, DnOP (also written DNOP), DIBP (also written DiBP), DnHP (also written DHEXP), DPENP (also written DnPP or DPP), DCHP. DEHT and DINCH appear only as context (non-phthalate substitutes).
- Matrices in scope: foods (fatty foods, dairy, oils, infant foods, fast food) and consumer products (toys and childcare articles, food contact materials, cosmetics).
- Approach: primary regulatory texts were opened and read for every limit. Occurrence figures come from the EFSA 2019 opinion and one peer-reviewed survey. Where a source could not be opened or a value could not be confirmed, the item is listed in section 7 as "not found".
- Wording rule: this file states measured values against stated limits only. It makes no product or health claims.

## 6. Gaps

1. Laboratory background contamination (sourced). CPSC-CH-C1001-09.4 states that phthalates are a common contaminant and that even low levels of contamination can impact quantitative results; it requires a laboratory reagent blank [S2]. In Edwards et al., BBzP, DnBP, DEHP and DEP were detected in 50% or more of laboratory method blanks, and DEHP, BBzP, DEP, DnBP and DnOP were detected in field blanks, which raised the MDLs [S13]. A certification scheme will need blank acceptance criteria; none are sourced here.
2. LOQ versus limit. EFSA removed 147 analytical results because reported LOQs exceeded the EU SMLs [S12]. Lowered SMLs (DBP 0,12 and DEHP 0,6 mg/kg [S5]) increase the demand on method LOQs. Published LOQs for standardized food methods were not found.
3. Recovery. Edwards et al. reported DEHP-d4 recoveries of 32 to 82% and median spike recoveries below 50% for DEHP (47%), DnOP (49%) and DiNP (24%) [S13]. Recovery criteria for an ICS method requirement are not sourced.
4. DINP and DIDP separation. Reg. (EU) 2023/1442 recital 11 states further validation work is still required before DINP and DIDP can be routinely differentiated in official controls [S5].
5. No numeric food concentration limits. None of the instruments read sets a maximum level for phthalates in food itself; EU limits are migration limits from plastic FCM [S5], and FDA actions concern authorizations [S6] [S7].
6. Occurrence coverage. No primary survey was opened for infant foods, dairy (as a dedicated survey), toys, or cosmetics. Nanni et al. and Sakhi et al. values are taken from the EFSA summary, not the original papers [S12].
7. REACH entry 52 text is from the 2009 adoption [S4]; later amendments to entry 52 were not checked.
